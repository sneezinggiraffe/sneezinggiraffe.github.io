# One More Task: design and release specification

Date: 2026-11-04
Scope: `onemoretask/index.html`, `onemoretask/game.js`

This is the current agreed design, including the phase, token, cluster and network revisions. It replaces the earlier eleven-phase target.

## Experience

Start with manual freelance work, delegate to AI, feel the cost of keeping it running, hire management, reorganise into clusters, then let the CEO build a network of more than one hundred nodes before retiring. Reveal controls gradually. Early tasks use a stable Work-button position; automation can naturally empty the queue. Desktop and mobile are both supported.

## Upgrade pacing and simplification

Up to three ordinary upgrades reveal together; some are deliberately unaffordable when first offered. Discover Free-Tier AI appears alongside them with a 50 tasks requirement and an Unlock button. It costs no cash and requires no earlier upgrades, guaranteeing at most 50 work clicks before AI even if productivity upgrades are skipped. Later phase transitions and the second subscription require all preceding upgrades. Phase 2 upgrades begin with Few-Shot Prompting and Marketing Campaign at 200 tasks; the $2,500 Upgrade to AI 2000 Basic requires 300. Phase 3 groups begin at 350, 425 and 500 tasks. Second AI Subscription follows all preceding upgrades, requires 650 tasks and costs $18K. The licence requires 750 tasks and costs $25K. Reputation is removed from state, rewards, UI and retirement conditions. The Phase 9 acquisition costs $1.5M.

Start with **Find Work**, then **Do Task**. Once the queue opens, **Work** starts a job. Post-It Notes and GTD Workflow let one click start 2 and 5 jobs respectively through Phase 3. Five is the manual limit. Noise-Cancelling Headphones instead reduce work by 10% for manual and AI jobs. Manual groups work in parallel at the normal per-job speed, retain individual pay and progress, do not expire, and survive reloads. Buttons show **Work on N** and **Working...**. A session starts only jobs already available; another click starts the next session after the current jobs finish.

Empty upgrade space preserves the height and bottom margin of the last visible card. Standard cards and the initial empty space use a 68px minimum card height.

## Two-agent transition

Phase 3 requires Second AI Subscription before Multi-Agent Licence becomes available. Both subscriptions have individual manual Top-up tokens controls. The licence converts them into normal agent cards while preserving loaded tokens and current jobs. AI 2000 Basic and AI 2000 Pro have matching token bars and buttons through Phase 4. The original AI 2000 Basic is explicitly labelled as an older, slower model. Selling and replacing agents becomes available in Phase 4.

AI 2000 Basic begins with a 50-token starter reserve and has a 40-token tank. AI 2000 Pro has a 100-token tank and consumes 12 tokens per working second before efficiency upgrades in Phase 3. After licensing, Basic keeps its 40-token tank and works at one quarter of its previous licensed speed. Pro joins the standard 200, 500 and 1,500-token worker capacity curve and uses the normal worker consumption rate, so it remains competitive with newer named agents. Manual top-ups, Manager distribution and cluster supply use those model rules. The Agents heading omits subscription-status and slot-count labels.

## Tasks and tokens

1. Automated tasks run at their natural speed. Completed cards disappear immediately so the player can keep clicking the first Work button. No forced minimum duration or completion delay remains.
2. Every agent and Manager uses “Top-up tokens”. A partial top-up transfers exactly the missing amount from reserve, even with a Manager online. The bar counts actual tokens, shown as integers. Top-up buttons remain enabled. tok/s is a five-second smoothed average of actual consumption, displayed with a minus sign in the top bar and company leadership. Remove Auto toggles and label dismissal Sell.
3. Standard worker capacities are 10, 40, 200, 500 and 1,500 across Phases 2 through 6. Basic retains its fixed 40-token capacity; Pro has 100 in Phase 3 and uses standard capacity after licensing. Base consumption rises from 1.2 to 4, 15, 60 and 150 tokens per working second. A fully busy Phase 5 standard worker needs a refill after about 21 seconds with all three early efficiency upgrades. Traits and efficiency apply. Eight fully busy Phase 6 agents consume roughly 29,000 tokens/minute after all three efficiency upgrades.
4. The Manager holds 4,000 tokens and supplies agents in small increments, including the original AI agent. An empty Manager continues assigning jobs to loaded agents. It cannot create tokens.
5. The Phase 7 unlock first reserves a Token Manager slot. A purchased Manager is required to enter Phase 7. The extra agent slot appears in Phase 8 and requires a purchased Token Manager. Its purchases use cash; unlocking the role alone does not enable purchases.
6. Token pack sizes rise with workload; prices rise 2% of the base price for each pack bought, including automatic purchases. Incident costs are determined at creation using earned wealth. Spending cash does not reset either price curve.
7. Before clusters, order roles Manager, Token Manager, then agents. Manager and Token Manager cards use a slightly lighter background. DevOps occupies a dedicated slot at the top of Incidents, including a dashed Buy DevOps card. It does not consume shared agent capacity. Unlocked Manager, Token Manager and DevOps roles have dedicated empty purchase cards. Locked roles remain absent.
8. Phase 8 simplifies agent cards by removing detailed stats and status buttons. Remove the dashboard. Tasks completed leads the top bar, followed by the other counters; token consumption is visible beside reserve tokens.

## DevOps

DevOps resolves warning and critical incidents automatically. Critical incidents consume twice the response time. There is no final player approval step. Manual and paid fixes remain optional shortcuts. Cluster construction includes management agents and DevOps software.

## Clusters, Phase 9 through 11

1. The existing workforce becomes Cluster 1 with management agents included.
2. Operations Blueprint precedes the Second Cluster Permit. The Third Cluster Permit requires a full second cluster. Three full clusters are required before Phase 10. Full-company upgrade visibility gates apply only in Phase 9. From Phase 10, upgrades remain visible while agents and clusters are being added.
3. Bring cluster online includes its $12K management software cost in the infrastructure price. Construction progresses through Procuring GPUs, Racking hardware, Installing software and Final checks. Afterward, click empty slots to provision identical $2.5K agents. Each slot fills with a four-second installation progress background, reduced to two seconds after CEO training. All eight slots remain visible; automated clusters later have sixteen. Utility agents consume no slots.
4. Each team's token balance serves its own agents. Top-ups spend reserve tokens; Token Managers make visible paid purchases.
5. Provisioning starts at $50K plus $12K included management software and rises with the number ever provisioned. Full spare clusters can be sold. Retain at least one cluster; selling never resets the next provision price.
6. The $1M AI CEO is a dashed purchase card above the clusters, not an upgrade-list item. Buying it expands every cluster from eight to sixteen slots. It provisions one agent every five seconds; Provisioning Pipeline ($600K) halves that interval. Agent batching upgrades follow Scale Mode. Scale Mode halves the interval again and takes over new cluster creation. Manual provisioning disappears.
7. Service upgrades deploy client retainer contracts at $80/s before downtime and $2/s upkeep. The footer shows current income after downtime. Two managed clusters increase agent task speed by 50%; three or more double it.

New clusters show the current hardware build stage and progress bar. Construction takes twelve seconds initially. Preassembled Server Racks halves that duration, CEO training halves it again, and Continuous Deployment fixes it at two seconds with two concurrent builds.

## Autonomous network, Phase 12 onward

1. Three managed clusters and 1,600 completed tasks unlock the autonomous operating model. Existing agents and loaded tokens convert to aggregate workforce and token balances.
2. Phase 12 compacts clusters into autonomous nodes. The CEO continues constructing and staffing them; manual cluster and agent controls are absent.
3. After twenty online nodes and at least nine additional nodes during Phase 12, Replication reduces costs by 35%. Global Expansion at forty completed nodes reduces costs another 40%. Costs rise with the number ever purchased.
4. Aggregate production consumes tokens and stops when depleted. Token Managers buy packs and distribute reserve tokens.
5. Phase 14 boxes show only short names such as C1 and C100. There is no fixed 128-node limit, and saved networks may exceed two hundred nodes.
6. Golden Parachute requires at least one hundred managed nodes. Node Replication appears once twenty shells exist and remains disabled until its target number of fully completed clusters is online. Additional clusters under construction do not block the purchase. Upgrade requirements show only a target such as “(20 nodes)”, without progress fractions.

## Early automation and hiring checkpoints

The original agent is AI 2000 Free in Phase 2. Upgrade to AI 2000 Basic renames it in Phase 3, where the lifestyle is Coffee Shop Regular. The second subscription is AI 2000 Pro. The income estimate includes the original agent using the same work-rate calculation as its production loop; empty tanks and idle agents add no estimated task income.

The first incident, whether triggered by a random failure or the twenty-second tutorial timer, introduces incidents exactly once. A timed introduction cannot also create a random incident in that tick. Prompt Engineering 101 appears in Phase 4 after at least three incidents have occurred, costs $3,200, and reduces failure rate by 20%.

The initial licence has three total slots. Buying the third specialist and buying Task Templates exposes the four-slot expansion, priced at $5,000. Parallel Prompting ($3,800, +15% speed) and Response Streaming ($5,200, +20% speed) follow that expansion and are required before six slots. Specialist roles may be bought repeatedly; Manager, Token Manager and DevOps remain unique. DevOps has its own slot in Incidents and does not consume shared agent capacity. This makes every advertised specialist slot usable.

Unlocked empty Manager and Token Manager positions are dashed purchase cards at the top of Agents. Those positions are reserved until bought. Each other empty slot has its own dashed card with direct buttons for buying each specialist type. Unaffordable purchases remain disabled. Buying a Manager costs $20,000, separately from its unlock upgrade.

Phase-entry task minimums are 50, 300, 750, 1,150, 5,000, 30,000, 150,000, 1,000,000, 1,200,000, 1,500,000 and 2,000,000 for Phases 2 through 12. Later transitions use node requirements.

Phase 5 gives its tools and operations upgrades more room: Web Search Tool costs $7,500, Sales Playbook $12,000, Code Execution Tool $16,000 and Revenue Ops System $28,000. Bulk Processing ($9,000) targets ten tasks per job. The active-player simulation checks that this phase gives its upgrades time to matter. This is a comparative pacing check, not a human playtime target.

Phase 7 shows upcoming upgrades before their task requirements are met, with prices and task targets visible. Purchases still require both the task milestone and enough cash. Its savings goals are Revenue Optimization ($150K, 45,000 tasks), DevOps Slot ($60K, 30,000 tasks), Incident Runbooks ($175K, 65,000 tasks), Delivery Pipeline ($250K, 90,000 tasks, +25% agent speed) and Premium Client Contracts ($350K, 115,000 tasks, +35% task pay). Operations Flywheel costs $1M and requires all preceding upgrades and at least 150,000 tasks. The deterministic journeys verify paid purchases throughout this phase before entering growth.

Empty agent slots offer one-click Buy Writer, Buy Researcher, Buy Coder, Buy Analyst and Buy Support buttons, with Buy Sales appearing in Phase 8. The card shows the per-agent price; each purchase button is disabled when unaffordable. There is no role dropdown. DevOps has a matching dashed Buy DevOps placeholder with its price and a Dedicated incident slot label. Full shared capacity cannot block that purchase.

Before clusters, player-facing actions use Buy for agents. Clusters use Bring cluster online and Provision agent. The Token Manager upgrade description is “Automatically buy tokens when they run out.” Purchasing now happens before tank transfers, with enough headroom to keep tokens above the 4,000-token warning threshold when cash is available. DevOps has a separate $60K unlock upgrade in Phase 7.

## Batch throughput and progression

The Multi-Agent Licence description is “More agents, more work.” Four-slot and six-slot expansions block later upgrades until purchased. Bulk Processing targets ten tasks per job in Phase 5, Auto-Assign Playbooks raises the target to 100 in Phase 6, Revenue Optimization to 500 in Phase 7, and Growth Marketing to 2,000 in Phase 8. Each batched job varies by ±20% around its target and stores its actual batch size when created. Phase 6 jobs deliver 80–120 tasks, so the completion counter's final digits change naturally. Completion adds the actual count without multiplying its cash payout. Job difficulty stops growing after the early fatigue ramp. No free task-count grants are used to reach one million before Cluster 1.

Cluster expenses are labelled Server power & cooling. Cluster construction includes management agents, then empty slots provision identical agents with an installation progress bar. No specialist-role purchase buttons appear inside clusters.

## CEO scaling upgrades

After Scale Mode, upgrades stay available throughout construction and staffing. Faster AI Provisioning ($40M) speeds both coordination and agent installation by 50%; Dual Agent Provisioning ($75M) installs two agents per cycle, followed by Four-Agent Provisioning ($100M). Parallel Cluster Builds ($150M) allows two concurrent builds. Preassembled Server Racks ($200M) halves construction time. Their costs spread purchases across the scaling phase instead of allowing the entire chain to be bought immediately.

Phase 11 adds Fully Fitted Racks ($80M): all sixteen agents install during construction, with their $40K cost included in each new cluster purchase. Phase 12 adds Continuous Deployment ($150M), completing two fully fitted clusters every two seconds when funds permit. Construction consumes real cash, respects its concurrency limit, and stays visible in the existing progress cards. Buying these upgrades uses the existing save-format-8 upgrade list and requires no new save schema.

Node-based progression counts fully completed clusters. A new construction project cannot disable an otherwise satisfied node target. Compact-node conversion preserves ongoing fully fitted rack construction without prematurely producing tasks.

## Company leadership and delivery

From Phase 10, token purchasing becomes one company-wide Token Manager service, shown beside the AI CEO in agent cards above the cluster grid. Cluster managers and DevOps remain local to each team. The Token Manager automatically supplies every managed cluster and shows reserve tokens, smoothed consumption and its latest purchase. Transfers continue in small increments: up to 250 tokens into each cluster and 25 into each agent per 100ms tick. Purchases still deduct cash and follow the existing pack-price curve. There is no additional manual Manager-refill requirement.

Cluster work capacity scales with the company: twelve task spaces and one arrival per spawn interval for each managed cluster. This removes the old twelve-task ceiling across the entire company. A three-cluster Phase 10 regression scenario consumes over 25,000 tokens in one minute while conserving total tokens across reserve, cluster and agent tanks.

An outlined Bring cluster online card occupies the next free grid position. It is disabled when unaffordable and hidden during construction, at capacity, or after Scale Mode is bought. Manual Cluster 4 appears once the original three clusters have at least twelve agents each. Cluster 5 follows when the existing clusters are fully online. Both must be full before Scale Mode appears. Cluster sale controls read Sell.

At two managed clusters, the board adds a Delivery panel with a ten-second smoothed completion rate, queued and in-progress counts, and three recent completions. Tasks remain expanded by default. A right-aligned Hide tasks / Show tasks chevron toggles detail and keeps keyboard focus. A phase change restores expanded tasks. From Phase 12 the panel shows the delivery rate, managed-node count and delivery revenue without individual task controls. Observation history and the expanded-list preference are session-only, reset on a new story, and do not alter the save schema.

## Ending

Buy Sell the Company and Retire Early! when there are at least one hundred managed nodes, debt is below ten, no unresolved incidents remain, and estimated income is greater than twice expenses. Settlement is current cash plus 120 seconds of estimated income.

Selling freezes the company and opens a separate golf-and-health ending screen. “Interesting, there's another model out…” starts a new cycle with permanent click power, starting tokens and the earned extra starting slot. Music stops, its preference survives, and the new cycle begins at the introduction and first manual task with returning-player story text. “Actually retire” completes the permanent ending, with only a confirmed new-story reset thereafter. Both ending stages persist on reload. Neither choice is offered before selling.

## Persistence and interaction

- Save format version 8 is a breaking change. There is no migration. Older or invalid saves are preserved until the player explicitly resets them. Storage errors have visible recovery controls.
- Offline earnings include recurring services and referral contracts at half efficiency, minus expenses, for up to eight hours. Losses are forgiven. Saved busy agents and aggregate nodes do not generate offline production. Offline awards are immediately saved.
- Story and settings dialogs pause foreground play. Background gaps follow the offline policy.
- Keyboard activation, stable focus during live updates, dialog focus management, zoom, reduced motion and small-screen scrolling are supported.
- Reset affects only this game's save. Page-exit autosave cannot recreate a deleted save.

## Completion and validation

The fifteen-phase arc must reach its persistent ending from a fresh state. Tests cover three deterministic journeys with no cheats or injected cash, sustained autopilot, token conservation, task visibility, managed-cluster purchases, one hundred or more nodes, retirement, storage failures, and lifecycle behavior. Browser tests cover actual purchase controls and desktop, portrait and landscape layouts.

Require 100% coverage of changed executable game lines relative to the documented release baseline. Run the relevant regression and browser checks, inspect screenshots, and complete three consecutive review iterations with no further fixes. See `onemoretask/README.md` for commands and release files.

Advanced inter-node routing topology, dynamic agent caps and custom CEO strategies remain outside this agreed release scope.
